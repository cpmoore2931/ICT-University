# Entry Models — PJ's Four, Dropped Into Page's ORB

Source: `claude/pj-trades-playbook.md`. This doc is the *how do I spot an entry*
sheet. The ORB rules live in `claude/page-orb-strategy.md`.

---

## The frame — read this first

PJ's whole model is one sentence:

> **Internal liquidity is where you ENTER. External liquidity is where you TARGET.**

- **External** = swing highs, swing lows, overnight highs/lows. Your target.
- **Internal** = the gaps *inside* the leg. Your entry.

So the order never changes:

1. Where is the untagged draw? (external — name it first)
2. What's the gap between here and there? (internal — that's your entry)
3. Enter at the gap. Target the draw.

**The disqualifier, every single time:** if the draw is already tagged, the trade
does not exist. Say it out loud before you click.

## The four entry models

He calls these his arsenal. That's it — four. Not forty.

### 1. FVG — Fair Value Gap

**What it looks like:** three candles. Candle 1's wick and candle 3's wick do not
touch. The space between them is the gap.

- Bullish FVG (gap left going up) = support
- Bearish FVG (gap left going down) = resistance

**Entry:** price comes back into the gap.
**Stop:** just past the far side of the gap.
**CE (consequent encroachment)** = the 50% line of the gap. Turn the middle line
on in your boxes. Many wicks holding CE = that gap has weight.

---

### 2. IFVG — Inversion ⭐ his most used

**What it looks like:** a gap that price **broke all the way through**. It flips.

- Bearish gap that price closes above → it is now **support**
- Bullish gap that price closes below → it is now **resistance**

**Entry: the retest of the flipped gap.** Not the break. The retest.
**Stop:** just past the gap.

This is his bread and butter. If you only learn one, learn this one.

---

### 3. Breaker block

**What it looks like** (bullish version), in order:

low → high → **lower low** (that's the sweep) → **new higher high** (that confirms it)

**How to draw it:** the **two highest up-close candles** before the leg that took
the liquidity. **Use the candle bodies. Not the wicks.**

**Entry:** the pullback into the breaker.
**Stop:** below the breaker.

Flip everything for shorts.

---

### 4. Unicorn ⭐ the best one

**What it looks like:** an **FVG sitting inside a breaker block.** Two things
stacked in the same place.

**Entry:** the pullback into the **FVG part** of the breaker.
**Stop:** below the breaker.

Higher probability than a plain breaker. When you see one, that's your A+.

## CSD — the confirmation step

**Change in State of Delivery.** After the sweep and the snap-back, price takes
out the swing high (for longs) on the other side. That tells you delivery flipped.

Order it goes: **sweep → V-shape → CSD → entry.**

You can read it on any timeframe. He'll even use a 30-second CSD.

## The timeframe stack — this is how he gets cheap entries

| TF | Job |
|---|---|
| 1h / 4h / daily | Context |
| **15m** | His favorite. Structure and the main gap. |
| **5m** | The zone. The gap you're trading usually lives here. |
| **1m** | Confirmation. Watch how candles close into the 5m zone. Long wicks = pressure. |
| **30s** | The trigger. A 30s inversion inside a 5m zone = tiny stop. |

**The pattern: 5m defines the zone → 1m confirms → 30s triggers.**
That's how a 5-point stop gets a 16-point target.

**This is the answer to "how do I stop entering late."** You don't guess a better
price. You go to a smaller timeframe and let it hand you one.

## Now — bolting this onto the ORB

Here's the key to the puzzle: **your ORB is already internal → external.** You
built it that way without knowing the words.

| Your ORB | PJ's word |
|---|---|
| Previous high (your target) | **External draw** |
| ORB midrange | **Internal — your entry zone** |
| Sweep of the ORB low | **Manipulation / money sweep** |
| Price snapping back up | **V-shape recovery** |

You were already right. You just had no **entry model** at the mid — that's the
hole, and that's exactly what you said your weakness was.

### The combined sequence

1. **Draw check.** Is the previous high untagged? No → no trade, go home.
2. **Sweep.** Price takes the ORB low. Watch it. Never trade this leg.
3. **V-shape.** Price snaps back up.
4. **CSD.** Price takes out the swing high made during the sweep.
5. **Reclaim.** Price closes back above the ORB mid.
6. **Find the model at the mid.** Ask, in this order:
   - Is there a **gap inside a breaker**? → **unicorn**, take it
   - Did a gap **flip polarity** on the reclaim? → **inversion**, take it
   - Did the reclaim leg **leave a fresh 1m FVG**? → **FVG**, take it
   - Did the sweep build a **breaker**? → **breaker**, take it
   - **None of them?** → **no trade.** No model, no entry.
7. **Enter on the retest** of whichever one you found.
8. **Stop** just past that gap or breaker.
9. **Target** the previous high.
10. Run the math box. Under 2:1, don't click.
11. Hands off. Let the market do the rest.

Step 6 is the piece that was missing. That's the entry.

## What NOT to use yet

**The front-run.** PJ enters *before* the inversion confirms when waiting would
wreck the RR. He calls the judgment behind it **"feel"** and says plainly it is
not learnable overnight.

**Page: leave this alone for now.** You said it yourself — you cannot train a
gambling feeling. Front-running is the one part of his model that is pure
discretion. Learn it in year two, after the four models are boring.

Same for BPRs. He says himself: *"I don't really even use BPRs in my checklist."*
Optional. Skip.

## The don't list — his rules, they apply to your ORB too

1. No trading the first 15 minutes of the open
2. Don't trade chop / barcode
3. **No untagged draw = no trade**
4. Never trade the manipulation leg
5. Don't flip your bias right after a stop-out
6. If you break a rule to get a trade on, that's not trading. That's gambling.

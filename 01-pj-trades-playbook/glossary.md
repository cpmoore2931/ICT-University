# Trading Vocabulary — PJ Trades / ICT + Futures Mechanics

Reference glossary for the terms in PJ's teaching and on the live trading floor.
★ = term PJ uses directly. ⚑ = added from the trade-review transcripts.

---

## 1. The master framework ⚑

**★⚑ Internal liquidity.** The inefficiencies *inside* a leg — fair value gaps, imbalances,
order blocks. These are your **entry zones**.

**★⚑ External liquidity.** Swing highs and swing lows, overnight highs/lows, relative equal
highs/lows. These are your **targets**.

**★⚑ Internal → External model.** PJ's actual bread and butter: price retraces into internal
liquidity, you enter there, you target external liquidity. The IFVG is just one of several
triggers used *inside* this frame.

**★⚑ Defense level.** How he explains why an imbalance works. A sell-side imbalance is where
bears defend — price returns to it, they swat it down, next leg lower. Buy-side imbalance is
the bull equivalent.

---

## 2. Inefficiency

**★ FVG — Fair Value Gap** (a.k.a. imbalance, inefficiency). Three-candle pattern where
candle 1's wick and candle 3's wick don't overlap. Bullish = support, bearish = resistance.

**★⚑ Sell-side imbalance / Buy-side imbalance.** His preferred phrasing for a bearish /
bullish FVG, emphasizing which side got overwhelmed.

**★ IFVG / Inversion.** An FVG price has displaced *through*, flipping its polarity. The
**retest of the inversion** is the entry.

**★ Displacement.** Fast, one-directional, big-bodied movement. Creates gaps, validates
inversions.

**★⚑ CE — Consequent Encroachment.** The 50% of a gap. Why the middle line is on in his
templates. He counts how many wicks hold CE as a measure of a gap's strength — 10–15 wicks
respecting CE over two hours means that gap has real weight.

**★ BPR — Balanced Price Range.** Overlapping bullish and bearish FVGs. He calls this an
**optional confluence** — "I don't really even use BPRs in my checklist." A *triple BPR* is
three overlapping imbalances.

**⚑ New week opening gap.** The gap between Friday's close and Sunday's open. Another draw.

**Volume imbalance.** Gap between candle *bodies* where wicks still touch.

---

## 3. Liquidity

**★ Liquidity.** Resting orders, mostly stops. "The market loves to draw to people's orders."

**Buyside (BSL)** above highs · **Sellside (SSL)** below lows.

**★ Draw on liquidity.** The pool price is magnetized toward. Your target. "Half the battle is
where are our draws?"

**★⚑ Untagged.** A pool that hasn't been hit yet — still live. **The disqualifier: if the draw
was already tagged, the trade doesn't exist.** He says this repeatedly.

**★ Sweep / raid / manipulation / "money sweep."** Running stops through a high or low, then
reversing. Step one of every setup.

**★ LRLR — Low Resistance Liquidity Run.** Stacked wicks with little structure between. Price
travels through these fast, which makes them prime targets. (**HRLR** = the choppy opposite.)

**★ Relative equal highs / lows (EQH/EQL).** Engineered stop clusters. Sweep bait.

**★⚑ Fraudulent sweep.** Price wicks above/below a level but gets **no candle closures**
beyond it. He still treats the level as a live draw — the resting orders are probably still
there.

**★⚑ Data wick / "Trump wick."** The spike high and low created by an economic release or a
market-moving tweet. The market tends to come back for **both sides**. Mark both.

**★⚑ Trendline liquidity.** Stops along a diagonal — a wedge or bear flag. He waits for the
break, then a 5m inverse to confirm.

---

## 4. Structure

**★ AMD.** Accumulation (don't trade) → Manipulation (don't trade) → Distribution (trade this).
Also called Power of 3.

**★ V-shaped recovery.** The sharp snap-back off the sweep. Required before looking for entry.

**★⚑ CSD — Change in State of Delivery.** Price takes out the swing high/low opposite the
sweep, confirming delivery has flipped direction. His confirmation step between the V-shape
and the entry. Can be read on any timeframe — he'll cite a "30-second CSD."

**MSS / CHoCH — Market Structure Shift / Change of Character.** Same idea, ICT's naming.

**BOS — Break of Structure.** Continuation break in the trend direction.

**★⚑ Barcode / "barcode central."** Tight sideways chop. "Death by a thousand stabs." Don't
trade it — wait for the break.

**★⚑ Dead cat bounce.** The weak retrace back into an imbalance before continuation.

**★⚑ Historical chop zone.** An old ugly range on the left of the chart. Acts as resistance,
and makes a good *exit* target because price bogs down there.

## 5. PD arrays & entry models

**★ PD Array.** Umbrella term for any inferred institutional level — FVGs, order blocks,
breakers, unicorns. A sweep landing *inside* one is a major confluence.

**Premium / Discount / Equilibrium.** Above 50% of a range = sell zone, below = buy zone.

**★ OB — Order Block.** Last opposing candle before displacement.

**★⚑ Breaker block.** Forms at a major swing after a sweep. Structure: **low → high → lower
low → new higher high** confirms a bullish breaker. **Drawn using the two highest up-close
candles before the leg that took liquidity — bodies, not wicks.**

**★⚑ Unicorn.** **A fair value gap sitting inside a breaker block.** Higher probability than a
plain breaker. Entry on the pullback into the FVG portion.

**Mitigation block.** Breaker-like, but formed without a preceding sweep.

---

## 6. Entry & execution ⚑

**★⚑ Front run.** Entering *before* the inversion technically confirms, when waiting would
wreck your risk/reward. Requires a close draw on liquidity and a clean rejection candle. He
calls the judgment behind it "feel" — explicitly not learnable overnight.

**★⚑ Continuation model.** After a pool gets taken and price reverses, it pulls back into an
internal imbalance and continues. Works best when the next untagged pool is **within ~50–100
points**.

**★⚑ Connecting flight model.** Both overnight pools (e.g. London high *and* Asia high) get
taken around the same time → market likely pulls back into an internal PD array. Cross-check
ES for weakness.

**★⚑ Base hit.** A 1:1 trade taken in a choppy regime instead of holding for the full draw.
"Base hits pay the bills." "You cannot go broke taking profits."

**★⚑ Cheap trade / cheap entry.** Small stop relative to target. His whole edge — most of his
stops are 5–36 points.

## 7. Correlation

**★ SMT — Smart Money Technique / Divergence.** NQ vs ES. One takes its overnight high, the
other fails to. Checked on the **15m** at overnight highs/lows. A *clue*, not a requirement.

**★⚑ "Big brother."** ES / S&P 500. He checks it to see what's really going on. If ES looks
weak while NQ ran, NQ likely gets dragged down.

**★⚑ Trading the weaker index.** He'll short whichever index has *not* yet swept its liquidity
while the other has.

## 8. Time

**★ Killzone.** Asia 8PM–12AM · London 2–5AM · NY AM 8:30AM–12:10PM · Lunch 12–1PM ·
NY PM 1:30–~3:34PM (ET).

**★ True Day Open / midnight opening price.** 12:00 AM ET. Above = premium, below = discount.

**★ 8:30 opening price.** The other mandatory manual marking.

**★ Overnight highs / lows.** Asia and London extremes — the liquidity built while the US sleeps.

**Macro.** ~:50–:10 of each hour, where algorithmic runs cluster. His 12:10 cutoff is the end
of the last pre-lunch macro.

**Judas swing.** The false early-session move that reverses. Same concept as manipulation.

**★⚑ "First 15 minutes."** 9:30–9:45. He does **not** trade it. "Highly manipulated, highly
confusing, loves to trap people."

## 9. Futures mechanics

**★ NQ** E-mini Nasdaq — **$20/pt** · **★ ES** E-mini S&P — **$50/pt** ·
**★ MNQ** Micro Nasdaq — **$2/pt** · **★ MES** Micro S&P — **$5/pt**. Tick = 0.25 pt on all.

**Tick.** Smallest increment: 0.25 points → $5 on NQ, $0.50 on MNQ.

**★ Month codes.** H=March, M=June, U=Sept, Z=Dec. NQH2025 = March 2025 Nasdaq.

**★ Continuous contract (NQ1!).** Stitched front months. Fine for study; he wants you on the
dated contract so the room sees identical prices.

**★ Rollover.** Switching to the next quarterly contract. Announced in Discord.

**RTH / ETH.** Regular hours 9:30–4:00 ET vs. Globex (Sun 6PM – Fri 5PM).

**⚑ DOM — Depth of Market.** The order-book ladder. He watches it for feel alongside candle
closures and volume.

**⚑ Bookmap.** Order-flow heatmap software. His head trader (Gaines) calls out key bookmap
levels that often line up with PJ's gaps.

## 10. Risk & business

**RR.** Reward ÷ risk. His floor is **1:2**; **1:1** in bad conditions; 1:3 on good days.

**R.** One unit of risk. **Expectancy** = (Win% × avg win) − (Loss% × avg loss).

**★ TP1 / TP2.** Staged exits. At 1:2 he either closes full, or takes one contract off and
**moves the stop to breakeven**, letting a runner go to the external draw.

**★ BE — Breakeven.** Stop moved to entry. Makes the trade risk-free.

**Slippage.** Worse fill than intended — brutal around news (he cites a 200-point Trump candle).

**★⚑ Copy trading.** Executing the same trade across multiple accounts simultaneously. He runs
three.

**Prop firm.** Funded-account provider. Pass an evaluation, split profits.

**★⚑ Eval / evaluation account.** The challenge phase. He deliberately traded evals during bad
price action to gather information without risking live capital.

**Trailing drawdown.** Max-loss threshold that follows your high-water mark up. The rule that
kills most funded traders.

**Daily loss limit / consistency rule.** Firm-specific guardrails.

## 11. PJ's phrasing

**★ "Draw."** Where price is being pulled.
**★ "Tagged."** Already hit. Untagged = still live.
**★ "A+."** Sweep + PD array + SMT + clean untagged draw.
**★ "Chop suey price action."** Consolidation. Don't trade it.
**★ "Being the liquidity."** Taking the retail trade and getting stopped.
**★ "Spooling."** Price starting to accelerate.
**★⚑ "Base hits pay the bills."** Take the 1:1 in bad regimes.
**★⚑ "Paying for information."** Framing a loss as the cost of learning what the market wants.
**★⚑ "Summer PA."** Low-follow-through summer price action. Size down, weather it.
**★⚑ "Weathering the storm."** Surviving a bad regime without blowing up or quitting.

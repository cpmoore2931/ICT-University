## Episode 0 — Prep / Mindset (before the mentorship starts)

**What this video is:** Not a lesson on charts. It sets the mindset for the whole series.

### The big ideas
- ICT designed this mentorship for his daughter: a **stripped-down model, few moving parts**. Simple on purpose. Too many tools = analysis paralysis.
- **Three stages:** Yearner → Learner → Earner. Everyone starts as a yearner.
- Taught on **index futures** (E-mini S&P, Nasdaq), demonstrated on TradingView. Concepts also work on forex. Micros ($5/handle on ES vs $50) let small accounts trade the same model.
- **Goal is NOT perfection.** Goal: understand how price is delivered, read intraday bias, find setups that repeat at least once a week.
- Setups repeat with similar characteristics — never identical carbon copies. You learn to recognize the family resemblance.
- **One good setup a week is enough.** ~25 handles/week on ES is the target frame. Income-based thinking: cover the mortgage, the car payment — not home runs, not beating Instagram traders.
- **No trade calls.** He marks levels to STUDY (where price may draw to), never as signals. Don't build dependency on the mentor — that stunts growth. Own your wins AND your losses.
- Core market lens introduced: markets move inside a **liquidity matrix — buy side and sell side**. He drew a line above a high ("buy stops above this high, I was targeting that") — price reaches FOR liquidity.
- Entries can be **forgiving**; what must be precise is the **directional bias and the target** (where price is reaching for).
- Demo vs live: demo skill doesn't automatically transfer. Live money makes every tick emotional. Winning can be as dangerous as losing (overtrading, overleveraging).
- Personal responsibility is everything. Skipping the drills and homework = reduced results. Rushing to live ammo before training = you hurt yourself.

### Rules to remember from this episode
1. Simple IS the edge. Few moving parts.
2. Base-hit mindset: 25 handles a week, one A+ setup a week.
3. Precise about direction and draw; forgiving about entry.
4. Price reaches for liquidity (buy stops above highs, sell stops below lows).
5. Own your results. No codependency on any mentor.

## Episode 1 — Elements of a Trade Setup

**What this video is:** The first real teaching. He hands over the full skeleton of the model in one pass — the exact sequence PJ teaches.

### Market basics covered
- Focus market: **Nasdaq futures (NQ)**. Also ES (S&P) and YM (Dow). NQ is faster and more aggressive.
- A **handle** = 4 ticks = one full point move. ES: $50/handle ($12.50/tick). NQ: $20/handle. Micros are 1/10th the size — small accounts can learn safely.
- Best morning window: **8:30–11:00 AM New York time**. Usually one setup in there.
- 1, 2, and 3-minute charts are best for finding imbalances on indices (HFT algos work below 3 minutes).

### The top-down process (do this in order, every time)
1. **Weekly chart** — before the week starts, ask ONE question: is this week's candle more likely to expand higher or lower? That's the weekly bias. Don't predict the close, just the direction of expansion.
2. **Daily chart** — this is where MOST of the analysis lives. Find old highs and old lows (swing points). That's where the liquidity is. Pick the **draw on liquidity**: where is price being pulled to? (Price is a paperclip; liquidity is the magnet.)
3. **Hourly / 15-min** — transpose the daily levels down. Watch how price behaves around them.
4. **1–3 min chart** — this is where the entry pattern forms.

### Price draws to one of two things
- **Liquidity**: buy stops above old highs, sell stops below old lows.
- **Imbalance (fair value gap)**: a spot where one candle moved so fast that price only got delivered one way. The gap = previous candle's high to next candle's low (or reverse). Price comes back to "rebalance" it.

### The setup sequence (bearish example — flip everything for longs)
1. Weekly bias says DOWN.
2. Market consolidates, then **drops first** — a sucker play. It trips breakout sellers' stops (sell side taken). This is manipulation.
3. Then it **runs UP through relative equal highs / an old high** — the buy stops get swept. Those forced buyers are the counterparty smart money sells to. **Before any big move down, expect a stop hunt on buy stops first.** (Write that in stone.)
4. Drop to the 1–3 min chart. Wait for a **break in market structure**: a short-term swing low gets broken.
5. That break leaves a **fair value gap** behind. Price trades back UP into the gap.
6. **That's the entry.** Sell short inside the gap. Stop above the nearby swing high (or the candle high).
7. Target: split the day's range with a fib — 50% is the line. Above 50% = **premium** (expensive). Below = **discount** (cheap). Short from premium, target the discount side: the nearest old low, sell stops, or an opposing imbalance.
8. **Take the closest target.** Low-hanging fruit. Don't get greedy aiming for the lowest low — the market can deny you.

### Key mindset lines
- "Learn to trust going short while the candle is going UP into the gap." Retail waits for confirmation and gets in late.
- You will always leave money on the table. Don't beat yourself up. Exits won't be perfect — that's normal.
- It's not "buying and selling pressure" — it's liquidity, and an algorithm delivering price to it.
- This pattern repeats **every single week**, many times intraday. Back-testing it yourself is mandatory — no shortcut.
- One good leg (his example: 100+ handles on NQ) beats scalping 3–5 handle crumbs.

### Homework he assigned
Go through e-mini charts. Find: liquidity run (stops swept) → break in market structure the other way → fair value gap → entry → nearest target. Log each one and how many handles it offered.

### PJ translation (same sequence, PJ's words)
Money sweep (stops taken) → break/shift → price returns to the imbalance (inversion zone in PJ's model) → cheap entry → target the draw. No untagged draw = no trade.

## Episode 2 — Internal Range Liquidity & Market Structure Shift (Jan 25, 2022)

**What this video is:** The anatomy lesson. Exactly what a real market structure shift looks like, what an order block really is, and the session times that matter.

### Picking your liquidity pools (15-min chart)
- Below an old low = sell stops. Above **relative equal highs** = buy stops.
- If there are relative equal highs sitting higher than a single old high, **use the equal highs** — that's the juicier pool.
- Mark these on the 15-min FIRST. Then drop to 3/2/1-min for the entry.

### Shift vs. Break (his words)
- **Market structure SHIFT** = intraday term. It just means price now likely draws the other way *today*.
- **Market structure BREAK** = bigger deal, can lead to multi-day movement.
- This mentorship trades the SHIFT.

### THE rule that makes a shift real
A swing high/low being broken means NOTHING by itself.
It only matters **after liquidity was just taken**:

- Sell stops swept below an old low → THEN a short-term swing HIGH gets broken = bullish shift.
- Buy stops swept above old highs → THEN a short-term swing LOW gets broken = bearish shift.

No sweep first = no shift = no trade. You **anticipate** the shift after the sweep — you never force it or front-run it.

### Entry mechanics (fine detail)
- The break candle only needs to TRADE through the swing point — it does not need to close through it.
- After the break candle closes, watch the NEXT candle: does it leave a **fair value gap**?
- If yes → price trades back into the gap → that's the entry. Boom.
- **Two stacked FVGs:** let price dip into the LOWER one first (sacrifice the better price), then enter when it comes back into the upper one. If it fully overlaps both and keeps going — trade is dead, stand aside.

### What an order block actually is (his definition, corrected for everyone)
- An order block = **a change in the state of delivery.** That's it.
- A series of consecutive down-closed candles = ONE continuous order block. The key price is the **opening price** of that series, extended out in time.
- While price respects that open, the algo is offering one side. When the open gets violated, the state of delivery flips — now the algo offers the other side and starts hunting the opposite liquidity.
- A HIGH-probability order block has three things: liquidity was just taken + a market structure shift + a fair value gap with it.
- NOT every down-close candle is a bullish OB. Context is everything.

### Why price moves (his model of the market)
- An algorithm delivers/offers price. Buying and selling "pressure" doesn't drive it — volume comes IN at prices the algo offers.
- "Short squeeze / bear squeeze" = retail's excuse when they can't explain the algo running stops.
- Slippage: market orders fill where the algo is offering, not where you clicked.

### Session times (all New York local time) — memorize these
- **Asia:** 7:00 PM – 9:00 PM
- **London:** 2:00 AM – 5:00 AM
- **New York:** 7:00 AM – 10:00 AM
- Mark the HIGH and LOW of each session. Price loves to sweep those before the real move. Also watch any intraday high/low formed just before the 9:30 equities open.
- **Trade window: 8:30 AM – 11:00 AM** (can stretch to noon).
- **12:00–1:00 = no trades. Problematic hour. Stay out.**
- Afternoon trend 1:30–4:00, setup usually 2:00–3:00 — but that's OUTSIDE this mentorship's scope.

### Internal range liquidity (the episode's title term)
= a short-term high or low (with stops behind it), OR an imbalance, sitting **inside** a price leg that price is retracing back into. That's what you trade against on the way to the external draw.

### The live trade he showed (lessons from it)
- Watched the chart, NOT the P&L number. Watching money tick up/down is the worst thing to do.
- Sat through ~$455 drawdown calmly because he had ALREADY decided the lower FVG dip was allowed. Decide what's permissible BEFORE entry, so wiggle doesn't shake you.
- His risk: ~3.25% on the trade; personal max 4.5%.
- Took the **low-hanging fruit** target (near edge of the opposing zone) and flattened instantly when touched.

### Homework he assigned
Back-test e-mini intraday charts, going back as far as data allows: find stop hunt → market structure shift on 3/2/1-min between 8:30 and noon NY. Annotate every example. Log handles offered, drawdown a position would have taken, how long the move lasted. Build a **study journal** — it's your proof and your encouragement when you hit a rough patch.
- Hunter analogy: you can't hunt deer until someone shows you a deer track. These annotated examples ARE the tracks. Repetition trains your eye.
- Most people fake the back-testing. That's why they fail. The back-testing IS the secret.

### PJ translation
Sweep = the money sweep / manipulation leg. Shift = his break. FVG return = the inversion-zone entry. Internal range liquidity feeding the run to external = PJ's "internal to external." Low-hanging fruit = base hits. Chart stays clean — no graffiti.

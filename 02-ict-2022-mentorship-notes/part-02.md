## Episode 3 — Review Examples: ES Jan 26 + NQ Jan 27 (short episode)

**What this video is:** No new concepts. He walks two real days through the exact checklist from Episodes 1–2, showing how the rules filter trades OUT as much as in.

### Example 1 — ES, Wed Jan 26 (2-min chart)
1. Mark the day's low and high. Drop a fib on that range → the 50% line = **equilibrium**.
2. Price runs up through old highs → now it's in a **premium** (expensive).
3. First break down below the old high: does it leave a fair value gap? **NO → no trade. Wait.**
4. It rallies again, breaks down AGAIN — this time there IS a break in structure WITH a fair value gap.
5. Price trades back up into the gap → short there.
6. Targets below equilibrium: a swing low, an imbalance, a more pronounced low. It hit them all.
7. ~4419 short → ~4382 cover. He logs it imperfectly on purpose — you don't need the whole range.

### Example 2 — NQ, Thu Jan 27 (start on 5-min, work down)
1. Relative equal highs + an old high above = buy side resting.
2. Price runs above the old high. Break down with a gap? **NO → still no trade. Keep waiting.**
3. It goes higher instead. Fine. The rules kept you out.
4. Eventually a swing low IS traded through → check the break candle: fair value gap? **YES.**
5. Price rallies back into the gap → short → reaches below equilibrium → hits target after target.

### The lesson inside the lesson
- The rules are specific ON PURPOSE. They force the market to **show its hand** first.
- Two "almost" setups failed the checklist before the real one appeared. Waiting IS the skill.
- **8:30 AM New York = "that starts the hunt."** That's when he begins looking for the old high/low to be violated.
- Setups are never photocopies — but the similarities repeat. Train the eye (deer tracks again).

### Study journal — what to log on every example
- How long from the market structure shift until price came back into the gap?
- How long from entry to target?
- How much heat/drawdown would the position have taken?
- Handles offered.
- Write notes in the empty space right on the chart screenshot.

### Homework
Use the weekend. Go back through more days. Collect more annotated examples for the journal. Don't rely on his charts — use your own.

## Episode 4 — Intraday Order Flow & Understanding the Daily Range

**What this video is:** How a trading DAY is built — the sessions, the times, the two entry patterns, and the afternoon trend. Plus futures housekeeping.

### Futures housekeeping (the boring but needed stuff)
- Contract month codes: **H = March, M = June, U = September, Z = December.** (ESH2022 = E-mini S&P, March 2022.)
- Contracts expire the **third Friday** of the delivery month. Don't trade past expiration.
- **When to roll:** about a week before expiration, watch **open interest** (barchart.com, free). Trade whichever month has the BIGGER open interest — that's where the liquidity is.

### Chart setup (non-negotiable)
- Charts set to **New York local time**. Always. Everything is calibrated to it.
- **15-min = the bellwether timeframe.** Key highs/lows, fair value gaps live here. Then drop to 5-min, then 1-min.
- Draw vertical lines to frame the day: morning session / lunch / afternoon session.

### The shape of every trading day
- **Morning session: 8:30 AM – 11:00 AM** (news drops at 8:30 — that's why). Be positioned before 11. Maybe ride into noon.
- **Lunch: 12:00 – 1:00 = NO TRADES. Not even demo.** Weird, dirty price action.
- **Afternoon session: 1:30 – 4:00.** An algorithm macro fires at **1:30** — that's the earliest afternoon entry. Look for the first swing high/low that forms after 1:30, then run the SAME stop-hunt logic as the morning.
- Into the close: **3:40, 3:50, 4:00** — market-on-close orders make price accelerate. Bullish days ramp hard into the close.

### Before 8:30 — your prep
On the 15-min, find the **first** significant swing high and swing low to the LEFT of 8:30. Just the first one — no digging back for days.
- Swing high = 3 candles, middle one highest. Swing low = 3 candles, middle one lowest. **Candle colors/closes don't matter.**
- Above the swing high: buy stops. Below the swing low: sell stops. That's the morning's hunting ground.

### Displacement (the elephant test)
When price takes a high or low, the move away must be **obvious and energetic** — like an elephant falling into a kiddie pool. Water flies. No doubt.
- Weak, lazy, anemic break = NOT displacement = no setup.

### The ONLY two patterns you need
Bullish version (flip for shorts):
1. **Stop hunt entry:** a swing low gets violated (sell stops bought) → buy program starts → **spooling** (algo keeps offering higher prices, volume doesn't matter).
2. **Displacement entry:** no swing low gets taken? Then wait for sudden displacement HIGHER → fair value gap forms → price dips back into the gap → buy.

That's it. No breakers, no 15 gimmicky names.

### Three drives pattern (bonus recognition tool)
Three swing highs stair-stepping into an old high = liquidity building with every push. Smart money already positioned. The third push doesn't even need to take the old high.

### Daily profiles (homework framework)
Classify every day in your journal:
- Morning trend → afternoon **continuation** (measured move: afternoon ≈ repeats the morning's range)
- Morning trend → afternoon **reversal**
- Morning **consolidation** → afternoon trend
Then tie it to the daily chart: what were the days before doing? If daily bias says the candle expands higher, hunt longs in BOTH sessions.

### Discipline lines
- One good trade in the morning? **Done. Don't trade the afternoon.** Practice in demo instead.
- Don't micro-scalp ticks with big size. Trade the real legs with small size.
- News/world events make price noisy → stand aside until the market smooths out.
- July and August are hit-or-miss for indices. Rest of the year is clean.

## Episode 5 — Market Efficiency Paradigm & Institutional Order Flow (ICT's "episode six")

**What this video is:** THE fair value gap rulebook. Exact candle-by-candle rules, where the gap must form, and internal vs external range liquidity.

### The market efficiency paradigm (the mindset)
- Don't trade patterns for patterns' sake. No indicators, no momentum readings.
- **Enter longs where retail sells. Enter shorts where retail buys.**
- Smart money looks at TWO things only: **time and price — and TIME comes first.** Algos run on time first (4:00 PM run example). Retail has no concept of time.
- Smart money cannibalizes the uninformed crowd's liquidity. That's the whole game.

### Bearish FVG — the exact rules (3 candles)
- Candle 1 makes the high. Candle 2 must trade BELOW candle 1's low. Candle 3 keeps going but its high never reaches back to candle 1's low.
- **The gap = candle 1's LOW down to candle 3's HIGH.** Candle 2 is where the gap lives.
- Label candles 1, 2, 3 in every back-test annotation.
- Paint-roller analogy: rolling down leaves porous spots; the roller must come back UP over the same spot. Price does the same — it re-delivers the missed side.

### Bullish FVG (mirror image)
- Gap = candle 1's HIGH up to candle 3's LOW.

### WHERE the gap must form (this is the filter)
- Only after a **run into liquidity** (above an old high / double top for shorts; below an old low for longs).
- Then displacement: the break candle should CLOSE past the swing point, energetically.
- **Displacement high + displacement low = the range. The FVG must be INSIDE that range.**
- **No FVG inside the displacement range = NO TRADE.** Wait, or check another market. This pattern forms somewhere every single trading day.

### Entry + stop mechanics
- Easiest entry: **limit order one tick above candle 3's high** (bearish). Removes all guesswork.
- Stop: above candle 1 (the swing high) or above candle 2's high — whichever your risk allows. New traders: use the wider one.

### Internal vs external range liquidity (his exit map)
- **Internal** = an FVG or short-term level INSIDE the current dealing range → first partial.
- **External** = stops beyond the range's edge (below the equal lows / above the highs) → final target.
- Sequence: partial at internal, exit at external. Below 50% (discount) is where bearish targets live.

### Extra nuggets from the Feb 3 NQ walkthrough
- 9:30 equities open = wild volatility. **Usually the first run at 9:30 is opposite the real move** (not always).
- His live example: ~120 handles in ~11 minutes. Multiple re-entries offered into the same gap — the pattern gives second and third chances.
- TradingView replay button = fine for students who can't watch live (he never uses it himself).
- Real-time CME data on TradingView ≈ $4/month.
- A green candle pushing up into your gap FEELS like it will keep going. Dozens of reps in the journal turn that fear into anticipation.

## Episode 6 — Daily Bias & Consolidation Hurdles (ICT's "seventh")

**What this video is:** How to hold a daily bias, what to do when there ISN'T one, and the intermarket "crack in correlation" tell.

### Daily bias, plainly
- On the daily: liquidity got taken (say, below an old low) → expect retrace into the range → premium/discount off the dealing range's 50%.
- Bias = which way the NEXT daily candle likely expands. Stay with that bias until the draw is reached.
- During a bearish leg: count the up-close candles — there are barely any. You don't need to be right every day. **Being right ≠ being profitable.** Embrace the gray area and imperfection.
- Trading WITH bias: normal size (his: 3.5%, max 4.5% — students should risk **under 1%, even ¼–½%**). Against bias: cut leverage way down.

### When there's NO bias (consolidation hurdle)
- Price parked at equilibrium = even ICT has no clean read. That's honest, not ignorance.
- What to do: don't force a daily-range trade. Go smaller — trade intraday liquidity pool to liquidity pool. Surgical strikes: take the handles and get out. Don't overstay.
- He also "demands more Intel" from how price acts after the 9:30 open.

### Picking the right 15-min level (refinement)
- If a swing low sits INSIDE another candle's range, use the outer extreme — that's where the liquidity really rests.
- Relative equal highs = what retail calls resistance = a magnet, not a wall. Price blasted through them.

### The crack in correlation (the big new tool)
Stack **Dow + Nasdaq + S&P** on top of each other.
- ES and NQ make a LOWER low… but the Dow REFUSES to. That crack in correlation = accumulation fingerprint. (It's a macro refusing to deliver lower — NOT "buyers stepping in.")
- **It only CONFIRMS an idea you already had** (old low swept + bullish shift expected). By itself it means nothing — he blew accounts in the '90s trading divergence alone. Narrative first, always.
- Works in reverse for shorts (one index refuses the higher high).

### Using a correlated market as the trigger
- NQ never printed an FVG that morning. **ES did.** The averages move in tandem, so he used the ES gap's timing to enter the NQ long. The setup forms every day — but maybe in the sister market. Check all three.
- Turtle soup (his version): a fake run above an old high that sets up the short.

### "Leader" orders (context, NOT homework)
- Old floor-trader trick (George Angel): toss ONE contract in just to feel how price treats it. Losing leaders = "premium paid for Intel."
- That's a 30-year veteran's tool. It explains the small red trades on his statement. Not something a student copies.

### Mindset anchors
- SMC copy-traders fail because they have **no narrative** — they see a level break but can't say WHY price should move.
- Realistic money: steady base hits ($6k/week consistently) beats a one-time $60k unicorn trade. Think "cover the car note," then let it grow.
- His no-losing-days habit is his skill, not a student goal. He promises to show losing days when they happen.
- You're here to make money — not to impress anyone. Trading for emotional highs makes the lows hurt double.

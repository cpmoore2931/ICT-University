## ICT Episode 32 — The Consolidation Day + The 3 O'Clock Setup

**What this video is:** How to recognize a chop day in advance, survive it, and take the ONE clean trade it offers — the 3:00–4:00 PM run-and-sink.

### Reading the day before: the outside day tell
- Yesterday = an **outside day**: higher than the previous day's high AND lower than its low, closing down.
- Extra clue: the sell-off stopped **one handle short of the old daily low** (3,856 vs 3,855). Fell short = unfinished business below, and the "double bottom" feel sucks in buyers.
- Outside day + down close + old low NOT taken = **expect a choppy, listless day next.** He said it before the open.

### Consolidation-day anatomy
- Morning sets an initial range (9:30-ish high and low). Then price just chops INSIDE it for hours.
- **The middle of the day's range = the magnet.** In the middle, everything is 50/50 — that's why mid-range trades get chopped to death. No edge there. Don't trade the middle.
- Short-term highs and lows get taken both ways all day — ugly, tradable-looking, account-shredding. The model printed NOTHING valid in there, again.

### The one clean trade: 3:00–4:00 PM
- On consolidation days the algorithm often does its real move **between 3:00 and 4:00** (rooted in the old bond-close; now it's market-on-close order flow).
- The shape: run UP through the range's relative equal highs (squeezing shorts' trailed stops, baiting breakout buyers) → smart money sells to that flood of buy stops → **sink back to the MIDDLE of the range.**
- He called the window, the level (3,933.25), and the shape hours ahead on Twitter — then shorted 3,940.75, took 4 off at mid-range 3,908.75, left runners toward 3,855.
- Target on this play is modest by design: **the middle of the range**, not the opposite extreme. Anything more is bonus.

### Dealing range discipline
Every trade idea gets judged inside its CURRENT dealing range (most recent meaningful high ↔ low). He still favored the deeper 3,855 sell side longer-term, but the day's trade was framed only on the day's range.

### Human notes
- His son traded the same reads live (real AMP account, $6k → $10k that week — one loss, recouped it). Proud-dad moment, and proof the reads transfer.
- Everything time-stamped on Twitter; nothing deleted. Same standing offer: verify, don't worship.

## ICT Episode 33 — The Week's Finale: "I Want to Know That I Know" (3,855 finally taken)

**What this video is:** Friday review. The weekly draw (3,855) finally got run. The lesson: pick the ONE level whose break truly changes the day, and wait for it.

### The gatekeeper level
- All morning = chop he refused to touch. His line in the sand: **3,915.25** (a 15-min swing low). "I don't want anything until that breaks."
- Why not the smaller short-term lows? Ultra-aggressive entries in a high-volatility regime = catching breaks that reverse and run the equal highs. **"I want to know that I know."** One meaningful level beats five maybe-levels.
- Break came ~10 AM (he'd even said on Twitter Spaces he wanted to be off-air by 10 because the algo would take the low around then) → gap formed on the 5-min → displacement → stair-stepped all the way through 3,872 and into **3,855** — the level he'd been naming for a week.

### The premium-rule NUANCE (important refinement)
- The retrace into the gap did NOT reach the premium of the big displacement leg — and that was fine. Why? **When price is in a hurry to reach a weekly draw, don't expect deep retracements.**
- Judge premium/discount on the SHORT-TERM dealing range (he framed it on the 1-min swing) — the gap WAS at/above that equilibrium.
- Don't be discouraged when a rule needs experience to flex. The model is the training wheels; reading conditions is the riding.

### Break-and-retest, corrected (again)
- He does NOT trade "support broken becomes resistance." He only trusts a broken level if a **fair value gap exists at it.** No gap at the old low = the level means nothing on the retest. The gap is what he keys off; the level just marks where the liquidity was.

### Confluence chain to the target
Level break (3,915.25) → FVG short → next old low → next FVG short → displacement → 3,855 delivered. **No single thing is the trade — the chain is the trade.**

### The afternoon (same playbook as always)
Low of day by 1:30 → PM session rallies back into the **premium of the day's range** and an old imbalance → 3:00 algorithmic spool into the close. His son took the shorts down and the long back up — live account, **$6k → $13.4k in three weeks** (explicitly "not typical," and he had dad's experience at his elbow).

### Quiet meta-lesson
He's not a one-trick pony and doesn't want students thinking the ONE YouTube model is all that exists — but discovery is the student's job. Find the examples yourself; that's what builds pattern recognition nothing else can.

## ICT Episode 34 — The Sunday Opening Gap + Fib the Impulse Leg (post-livestream review)

**What this video is:** Review of a live-streamed morning. Two new tools: the Sunday opening gap as an all-week dynamic level, and the correct leg to anchor a fib on.

### The Sunday opening gap (new tool)
- Mark the gap where the futures session reopens **Sunday 6:00 PM NY**. Extend BOTH edges across the entire week.
- It behaves as a **dynamic level all week**: price ABOVE it → it acts as support; price BELOW it → it acts as resistance. He watched price cross it, retest it, and bounce off it repeatedly all day.
- Some weeks price runs away and never comes back — fine. But in **consolidation weeks (like FOMC week), price revisits it over and over.** Perfect chop-week compass.

### Perfect delivery exhibit
A 5-min FVG he called live: candle low 3,926.75 → the retrace came back to EXACTLY 3,926.75 as the next candle's high. To the tick. That's algorithmic delivery, and it's why he keys off gaps, not levels alone.

### The level chain that day
Relative equal highs + the Sunday gap edges + his called 3,953 objective (actual high: 3,954.50 — off by 1.5 handles). Morning was sloppy (few clean mechanics — recognize that and expect less); afternoon turned clean and delivered the full chain: gap retests → order blocks → run to the objective.

### The week's next draw
An untraded ACTUAL gap on the 15-min above (missing data) = "that won't be left open." Draw marked to the candle-low boundary of it. Above that → deeper daily run toward 4,000. Map named before the move, as always.

### Fib the IMPULSE leg (the correction he made for Hannah)
- Common student error: measuring the counter-direction leg (high-to-low) when hunting longs.
- **Rule: anchor the fib on the impulsive price swing that moves IN THE DIRECTION you want to trade.**
- Hunting longs → fib the LOW-to-HIGH up-leg → wait for the retrace into the discount (below 50%, ideally into an FVG) → buy.
- Hunting shorts → fib the HIGH-to-LOW down-leg → sell the premium retrace. Never measure the wrong leg.

### Livestream boundaries
Live sessions will stay RARE — on purpose. Regular hand-holding sessions would breed codependency, the one thing he keeps refusing to create. Failing on your own charts is part of the training.

## ICT Episode 35 — Mean Threshold, High Resistance Runs, and SMT Timing (pre-Memorial Day)

**What this video is:** Bullish week review into the daily bearish order block, with three refinements: the order block's three sensitive levels, when SMT shows up, and which runs NOT to expect.

### The order block's three sensitive levels (working INTO a bearish OB)
On the daily bearish order block candle, watch: **the LOW → the OPEN → the MEAN THRESHOLD (50% of the candle).**
- Price consolidating just under the OB then digging up through level after level = a compressed spring ("like a bomb waiting"). **Mean threshold TAKEN = bodes well for continuation** through the rest of the candle and the short-term high above.
- (The OB's high stays the least likely level — same ladder as before, now climbed from below.)

### Which leg to fib (two-stage delivery)
When a move up comes in TWO stages, the dealing range = the **most recent stage** (its short-term low to high) — not the whole run. Fib that, wait for the discount FVG, buy. He showed the market doing this repeatedly: dealing range → discount → rally → new dealing range → discount → rally. Stair-steps of accumulation.

### SMT — WHEN it appears (timing rule, new)
- SMT divergences form around **8:30, 9:30, and news releases** — the volatility injection points.
- Pattern he taught: **a nice run completed BEFORE ~7:00 AM → expect a deep retracement → then look for SMT at 9:30 to launch the next leg.** (That morning: NQ made the lower low at the retrace; ES refused = accumulation → 9:30 exploded.)
- Warning: without a bias, "SMT" appears and dissolves constantly. It confirms; it never leads.
- TradingView how-to: compare symbol plotted on the LOW (not close) when comparing lows.

### High resistance liquidity runs (why no shorts up here)
- Above price sat a thick stack of overlapping old ranges, down-close candles supporting, rebalanced zones. Punching DOWN through all of that would be a **high resistance liquidity run** — low odds, requires massive intent.
- Bullish underlying order flow + heavy overhead structure to fight = price goes up to KEEP going up, not up-to-reverse. Don't pick tops into that. (Clean, unopposed paths = the runs that actually deliver.)

### Holiday-week rules
- Friday before a long weekend: light volume — morning session ONLY, leave the afternoon to the gamblers.
- The holiday Monday itself: **no trading. Hot dogs and family.** He took his own advice.

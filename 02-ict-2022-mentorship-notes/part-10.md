## ICT Episode 40 — Weekly Bias + The Economic Calendar Filter (USDCAD walkthrough)

**What this video is:** The daily-bias question answered for the last time — by replacing it. Plus the full forex example with London close rules.

### The bias simplification (his final answer)
- **Everyday bias is unrealistic. Stop wanting it.** He doesn't have it either.
- Instead: determine the **WEEKLY expansion direction** — on the weekly chart, is price gravitating to an old high, an old low, or an imbalance? You only need the DIRECTION of the week's big energy, never the weekly close.
- Then filter WHEN to trade with the **economic calendar**: only days with **high or medium impact news** for YOUR market. Red and orange only. That's when the algorithm moves.
- Weekly direction + calendar day + killzone + the model's entry = the whole plan. All other days: you have permission to NOT know and NOT trade. "They don't give gold medals for overtrading, but they do blow accounts."

### The USDCAD trade (model on forex again)
- Daily: swing high formed AT an old high (3-candle swing — no 5-candle fractals, "you missed the boat") → next day bearish.
- 8:30 CAD retail sales = the volatility injection. Judas rally into an FVG + buy-side sweep BEFORE the news → displacement breaks the swing low AT the release → gap left behind.
- **Elongated gap rule:** when the imbalance is tall, fib IT — sell only in its upper half (its own premium). Stop above the gap candle. ~12 pips risk is fine — forex is scalable.
- Waiting for the news to hit before entering = the conservative path he wants students on. Front-running the release takes experience.

### London close (new time rule)
- **10:00–11:00 AM NY = London close profit-taking hour.** Have ~80% of a directional trade OFF by then — the opposing end of the daily range usually forms in that window.
- CAD + crude-inventory days: widen the window to 10:00–noon.

### Risk-on/risk-off as the bridge
- Bearish USDCAD = dollar down = **risk ON** = indices and foreign currencies rally easier. He used a market he wasn't trading (USDCAD had the news) to frame the ES long he took.
- Pair-picking with SMT: news on the euro? Compare EURUSD vs GBPUSD — trade whichever REFUSES to make the lower low (relative strength leader).
- London stat: when your directional bias is right, **London makes the high or low of the day ~70% of the time.**

## ICT Episode 41 — FINAL: Risk Management, Stop Management, and the Send-Off

**What this video is:** The last teaching video (41 of 41). The money-management math that completes the model.

### The day's honest lessons first
- His 3,805 call didn't print — stopped out at +2 (covered costs). After a wrong read around a Fed event: **close the laptop, come back after lunch.** No revenge.
- FVG stacking rule: a SMALLER gap in front of a LARGER one tends to get overrun so price can reach the big one. Target the far edge.
- A gap without meaningful displacement below the low = NOT a valid setup (he skipped one on camera again).

### Position-size math (the one worked example)
1. Account $10,000 × 1% risk = **$100 max loss.**
2. Trade needs a 9-point stop → $100 ÷ 9 = ~$11 per point allowed.
3. ES mini = $50/pt → too big. **Micros = $5/pt → 2 micros.** ($90 risked — under 1%. Good.)
4. Target 25.5 points away → ~$255 → about **2.5R**.
- Google "lot size calculator" — this is basic homework, not mentor questions.
- Compounding: 1% a day ≈ **22%/month** (not 20 — compounding). 1% a day forever is unrealistic; 5% in one good weekly trade averages the same.

### The drawdown ladder (write in stone)
- Full 1% loss → next trade risks **½%**. Another loss → **¼%.** That's the floor.
- You must earn back **50% of the loss** before returning to full risk.
- Three straight losses this way = only −1.75% total. Compare: doubling risk after losses = the death spiral (1% → 2% → 4% → shame → blown account).
- Reframe: **drawdown is a LOAN you'll collect interest on.** A losing trade is a flat tire — costs time and money, you still arrive. "Losses are a tax on success. Everyone pays the ferryman."

### Stop management (the static rules)
- Hard stop ALWAYS. "I don't use a hard stop but I know where I'd get out" = you don't know what you're doing.
- Price reaches **50% of the expected range** (entry → target) → stop moves up **25%** of the entry-to-stop distance. A cookie, not a leap.
- Price reaches **75% of the expected range** → stop to **BREAKEVEN.**
- Never jam to breakeven early. Watch order flow (down-close candles supporting, stop runs resolving higher) — being "onside" is what you monitor, not the stop.
- Two-order variation (at 2% total): same entry/stop twice — order #1 targets the near objective, order #2 holds for the day's range. His example: 2.25% banked + up to 4.5% more.

### The send-off
41 videos. Six months of honest work → you'll see what you never saw; a full year → it's yours like riding a bike. It won't be perfect — losses, missed moves, wrong reads forever — but it repeats more than it fails, "and I'm challenging you to determine whether that statement is true."

## BONUS (2023) — "One Setup For Life": Session Liquidity Ranges (Page's favorite)

**What this video is:** A later lecture that names exactly WHICH highs and lows ICT keys off intraday. The answer to "how does he pick the right level?" — all time-based ranges, all NY time.

### The ranges (memorize these five)
1. **Previous day's PM session: 1:30–4:00 PM** high/low (REGULAR trading hours — RTH filters out all overnight noise). First reference at the next open.
2. **London raid: 2:00–5:00 AM** high/low (electronic hours — you must see overnight for this one).
3. **Opening range gap:** RTH close vs the 9:30 open — the gap between them. (Different from the "opening range" = the first 30 min, 9:30–10:00.)
4. **New York lunch: 12:00–1:30 PM** high/low ("the 90-minute lunch"). The 1:30 macro raids it.
5. **AM session: 9:30–noon** high/low — referenced in the same afternoon or the next day.

### How to use them (the routine)
- At 9:30, ask: which range is price in **close proximity** to? PM range first → else London range → else the AM range from yesterday. That's the pool in play.
- **Bullish?** You WANT the sweep of the PM lows / lunch lows / London lows — that's smart money absorbing sell side to buy. Then target the opposing range's buy side or the opening range gap.
- Bearish = mirror everything.
- Fed-testimony / speech mornings: skip the AM circus entirely, trade the PM session — the cleanest setup of the day forms after 1:30.
- Toggle RTH vs ETH deliberately: RTH for PM/AM/opening-gap references, ETH for London. Most retail never even knows the toggle exists.

### Two reasons price EVER moves (his bluntest summary)
- UP: to take buy-side liquidity, or to reach an inefficiency (gap) above. That's it.
- DOWN: sell-side liquidity, or an inefficiency below. That's it.
- Sideways: the calendar told you it would consolidate. There is no third thing. Not pressure, not patterns, not indicators.

### New PD arrays introduced here
- **Inversion fair value gap:** a gap price trades THROUGH, then flips — an old bearish gap becomes support above (or old bullish gap becomes resistance below). He called one live on a 15-SECOND chart and price respected it to the tick.
- **Rejection block:** the lowest CLOSING price in the swing low — price can trade below it without taking the actual low. Sweep of the rejection block can be enough; the low survives.

### "One setup for life" (the thesis)
- Pick the ONE PD array your eye finds effortlessly (FVG, OTE, order block, breaker, turtle soup, inversion FVG…). That + these five time ranges + weekly draw = a setup that appears somewhere EVERY day.
- Caveat: maybe not in YOUR market today — that's why you watch the triad (ES/NQ/YM, or EURUSD/GBPUSD/DXY). It's always in one of them.
- Tools like Bookmap/DOM become redundant — the liquidity that matters is at these time-defined ranges, not every blob on a heatmap.
- Timeframes are fractal all the way down: he executed and pyramided off 15-second candles with partials at each liquidity high. Price is price.

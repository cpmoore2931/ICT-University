## ICT Episode 12 — Market Structure for Precision Technicians (ADVANCED)

**What this video is:** The deepest lesson so far — his real market structure model. Even his paid group saw it here first. He says himself: nobody learns this in one watch. Absorb it in layers.

### The only two questions that matter
Before sitting at the charts, ask:
1. Is price likely going UP — for buy stops, or to rebalance an old imbalance?
2. Is price likely going DOWN — for sell stops, or to rebalance an old imbalance?

That's the entire narrative. The algorithm only does two things: **run liquidity, and balance/rebalance imbalances.** Nothing else. (His riddle: if trendlines, Elliott, harmonics, supply/demand were real — how would the market know WHICH religion to follow each day? It can't. They aren't the mechanism.)

### The three ranks of highs/lows (nested, from Larry Williams, then extended)
- **Short-term high (STH):** any 3-candle swing high. (Old-school mark: one halo)
- **Intermediate-term high (ITH):** a swing high with LOWER swing highs on both sides — OR (his own discovery, the main one) **the high formed when price trades up to rebalance an imbalance.** (Two halos)
- **Long-term high (LTH):** the big one, usually tied to a higher-timeframe level like a daily FVG boundary. (Three halos)
Same three ranks for lows.

### The rules these ranks give you
- **Daily chart is the PARENT. All lower-timeframe swings are its children.** They obey it.
- Bearish? The long-term high must hold. The intermediate-term high must hold. If an ITH gets violated after your idea forms → **your idea is wrong. Stand aside. Don't force a re-entry.** That one rule keeps accounts alive.
- **Weakness tell:** an ITH that FAILS to rise above the short-term highs beside it = the market is very weak. (He anticipates the failure swing before it happens with this.)
- The rebalance-creates-an-ITH rule works both ways: gap fills going down in an uptrend → that low is an intermediate-term LOW → should never trade back below it while the move is on.

### Order block — corrected definition (again, deeper)
- NOT "the last up-close candle." It's the **whole consecutive series of up-close candles** (bearish OB) or down-close candles (bullish OB).
- The hourly OB's range = your hunting ground: drop to lower timeframes and find the FVGs INSIDE it.
- Aggressive entry: price returns into that OB range + hits an internal imbalance → enter. **No swing-low break needed** when advanced structure already says the market is weak.
- Classic (safer) entry: wait for the break down, then the return to the OB/FVG.

### Institutional order flow (the trade-management compass)
- **Bearish move: up-close candles are your resistance.** Price may tap back into them (that's the OB acting) but should NOT close through them.
- **Bullish move: down-close candles are your support.** Same idea flipped.
- This tells you when to HOLD (the candles keep holding) and when the move is dead (they fail).

### Targeting with structure
- Use the long-term high → long-term low range and project it.
- Or: anchor the fib at the **ITH where the decline actually begins** (the failed retracement), down to the long-term low. Project **-1 to -1.5 standard deviations** → generic price targets. His projection nailed the low.
- This answers the eternal "which swing do I anchor the fib to?" — the swing where the move was BORN.

### High-probability filter (simple and brutal)
- Can you frame the idea EASILY one direction, and only with a hard stretch the other way? → high probability.
- Can you argue it both ways comfortably? → NO trade. That "what the heck, I'm here anyway" entry is the account-killer everyone knows.

### The closing lesson: NEUTRAL is a position
War headlines, objectives already hit, price back above the old low → he declared himself **neutral and flat in every market for the rest of February.** Publicly saying "I don't know" is honesty, not weakness. Knowing when to do NOTHING is the trade.

## ICT Episode 13 — Advanced Price Action Theory IN ACTION (bonus episode)

**What this video is:** Saturday bonus. He takes Episode 12's advanced ideas and runs them live (demo) — one trade, +21% on a hypothetical $10k micro account — to show the simplified language does the same job.

### The setup he traded (bullish version of everything above)
1. Bias context: price wouldn't go lower — algorithm holding it up → the draw = buy stops above relative equal highs (retail "resistance" = the magnet).
2. Hourly bullish order block = the down-close candle series. Price above it, leaving an **imbalance** = high-probability OB confirmed.
3. High-probability bullish OB recipe: **down-close candle(s) + fair value gap + narrative reaching for buy side. That's ALL.** No engulfing patterns, no extra conditions.
4. 9:30 open: the first move is the fake (Judas). It dipped into the OB + FVG at a discount (below 50% of the leg) → long.
5. Once the gap filled: that low = **intermediate-term low → must not be taken out.** License to hold.
6. Every pullback into a down-close candle series = smart money buying again = his scale-in points.
7. Exit into the buy stops above the equal highs — where trapped shorts' orders are the counterparty.

### Trailing stops the right way
- Trail BELOW the most recent down-close candle SERIES (the whole order block), never inside the middle of the pullback.
- Stop inside the pullback = stopped out prematurely, then too scared to re-enter. That's infancy, and it's curable with reps.
- If price DOES close through the down-close candles — good, the stop did its job; the move was failing.

### Pyramiding (position building)
- **Biggest position FIRST, smaller each add: 3 → 2 → 1.**
- Never invert it (1 → 2 → 3 = a pyramid balancing on its point — wobbly, one wiggle wrecks it).
- All the scale-ins = ONE trade, one idea, one objective.

### Pacing and protection rules
- **20%/month is the aspirational GOAL frame** — a target, not a promise. Compounding does the heavy lifting; percent matters, not dollars.
- **Six months of consistent, documented demo trading** before live money even becomes a conversation. He rushed to live in 1992 and got wrecked on trade one.
- One good trade a week, then STOP. Scratch the remaining itch in demo. That's how he protects a green month during dangerous conditions (war headlines = live account stays parked).
- Butterflies in your stomach when entering = you're not ready. Indifference to any single outcome comes from a written plan: entry, stop, risk, targets, partials — ALL decided before entry, never during.
- Trade plan must be written. Retail decides inside the trade; that's what he did in '92 too.

### The quiet flex worth remembering
"If you win with Elliott waves or harmonics, my stuff was underneath the win. When your trade fails, my stuff was missing." Test it in the charts, not in the comments.

## Lesson — Multiple Setups Inside Each Session (AM + PM)

**What this video is:** How one trading day offers several entries, the swing-projection fib on candle BODIES, and Friday afternoon logic.

### The day, split
- Morning session: 8:30 – noon → usually **2–3 setups**.
- Lunch 12–1: NO new entries. (Taking profits is fine — a resting limit target CAN fill during lunch while your stop sits at breakeven.)
- PM session: 1:30 – 4. His cap: **max ~4 trades a day — 2 morning, 2 afternoon.**
- Each round of: displacement down → new FVG → retrace into it = another entry in the same direction. Miss one, the next bus comes. **Setups run on a schedule like buses — nobody has the right to be angry about missing one.**

### Swing-projection targeting (the fib-on-BODIES rule)
- Anchor the fib on the **highest OPEN or CLOSE (body)** of the swing high and the **lowest body** of the swing low. IGNORE wicks — wicks are distractions; the algorithm reads bodies.
- Project that range below the broken low (the "fulcrum point").
- His example: projection said 4501.25… the actual daily low was 4501.25. It converged with the daily bullish order block (4504) AND relative equal lows. **Precision only means something when the narrative layers agree.** A fib alone is noise.

### Why support/resistance "sometimes works"
- An old high, once BROKEN, becomes a discount array — it can act as support — but ONLY when the liquidity narrative calls for price to come back through it. That's why retail S/R hits sometimes and fails randomly: they have the level without the reason.

### Friday PM logic (new)
- Hit the weekly/daily objective in a DISCOUNT on a Friday? Expect the retrace back toward PREMIUM into the close (position squaring, short covering).
- Short covering = smart money BUYING → they run price up into the **PM-session buy-side liquidity pool** (the stops above the intraday high where shorts protect themselves).
- Engineering liquidity both ways: the sweep below relative equal lows sold to breakout sellers — their buy stops become the afternoon's fuel.
- He personally does NOT hold over the weekend in this climate (gap risk).

### Back-testing sermon (the strongest version yet)
- Back-testing = **discovery mode**, not perfection mode. You WILL do it wrong at first — that's the process.
- Annotate every chart with positive self-talk, as if you saw it coming. NEVER write negative comments ("I'm so dumb") in the journal. The brain files these reps as real experience.
- Do this for a minimum of a YEAR, and keep journaling even after going live.
- Your **unique model** = the one repeating context YOU recognize best. Two of his students can trade the same day in opposite directions and both be right by their own models.
- Two transactions can print a candle. Volume/"pressure" is a myth; the algo reprices and someone transacts.

## Lesson — Applying the Model to Forex (EURUSD walkthrough)

**What this video is:** The full model run on EURUSD, plus the forex-specific rule differences.

### The narrative (daily chart)
Buy stops above relative equal highs taken → bias flips bearish → the draw = the sell side below TWO sets of relative equal lows + the **1.09 big figure**.

### Institutional price levels (forex)
The **00, 20, 50, and 80** levels of each big figure. Lots of real business transacts there — they act as magnets and reaction points.

### Same targeting method
Fib anchored on candle BODIES (highest open/close → lowest open/close), standard deviation projections — his levels (1.0919, 1.0901…) converged with the 1.09 figure. Day's low: 1.0901. Projections only count once the fulcrum low is actually broken.

### Forex-specific clock rules
- **NY open killzone for forex: 7:00–10:00 AM** (indices stay 8:30–11:00).
- Opening price references: **midnight NY open** for the day + **8:30 open** for the NY session. When bearish, use whichever is LOWER as the minimum line the Judas swing must poke above. (Flip for bullish.)
- A high-impact news driver at/after 10:00 (ISM 10:00, crude inventories 10:30 for CAD) EXTENDS the killzone to ~11–11:30.
- He doesn't care what the news number MEANS — only that it injects volatility the algorithm uses to reprice.

### Entry refinements shown
- Bearish order block: price returns to the **BODY** of the last up-close candle — annotate bodies, not wicks.
- ITH confirmation again: swing high + imbalance + bearish narrative = a high that should not be taken out.
- The pre-news pump into the OB = designed to run shorts' buy stops right before the real move. Expect it; don't let it shake you.

### Exit "fluffing" (forex-specific but wise everywhere)
- Don't aim for the exact level. Cover **3–5 pips before** the target (10 pips when new). Spreads can widen against you at the worst time; overzealous limit orders miss perfect exits by a hair.
- Example trade math: ~10 pips risk, target at the figure → **8:1 reward-to-risk** — one trade like this passes a funded-account challenge frame.

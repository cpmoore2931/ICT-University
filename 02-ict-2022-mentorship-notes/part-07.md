## ICT Episode 27 — Friday PM Counter-Trend + The Self-Talk System

**What this video is:** A Friday-afternoon counter-trend long walked through with real (paper) executions, plus his deepest explanation of WHY the journal self-talk works on the brain.

### "Too clean" levels
- Relative equal highs at 12,553 — he flagged them in advance as "too clean." Clean, obvious levels don't survive; they're liquidity. That was the draw.
- **The draw does NOT need to be reached for the trade to pay.** You just need a setup that participates in the expansion TOWARD it. (He never got 12,553 — still booked the trade.)

### Friday afternoon squeeze logic (context for counter-trend)
- Down all week → Friday = natural retracement day. Shorts want to square before the weekend; the algorithm squeezes them into the close.
- Sequence he traded: 9:30 manipulation takes the overnight London low (sell side purged) → rally → **12:10ish lunch retracement** → **1:30 the algorithm seeks liquidity again** → price drops to the day's equilibrium and into a fair value gap BELOW the 50% (discount ✓) → ~2:00–2:25 accumulation in the gap + order block → displacement up → paid.
- Same model, counter-trend wrapper: buy a discount FVG when the day is bullish, even if the daily chart is bearish — because the purge already happened and position-squaring is the fuel.

### Execution notes (including his own mistake)
- Bought 36 (paper) inside the gap + order block, scaled out in pieces: 10 into strength, intended a LIMIT above a level for 20 more — but fat-fingered a MARKET close from his phone while out of the house.
- Lesson repeated by his own error: **phones are for managing, not executing.** Real platforms differ from TradingView; know your tools.
- Kept 1 runner "in case of a freak parabolic run" — that's fine once the trade is funded.
- Partial rule shown again: take a **premium profit scaling** — something off above the 50% of the low-to-high leg.

### The self-talk system (the psychology engine, fully explained)
- In every back-test annotation, write as if you SAW it coming: "I expected this drop into the gap, I wanted the displacement, it delivered…"
- Yes, it's technically lying to yourself — that's the point. Your subconscious stores the image PLUS your words as a **pseudo-memory**.
- Re-reading your journal on weekends replays those memories in your own voice. Over time this activates the **reticular activating system** — like buying a car and suddenly seeing that car everywhere. The charts become meaningful, so your brain flags the pattern live: "I remember this."
- He built this method from coping skills that beat his own agoraphobia and panic attacks. It's not a gimmick — it's trained pattern-sensitivity.
- Rules for the journal: rich annotations, your personality in the chart, positive words ONLY, screenshot and store everything.

### Proof-of-concept segment
- Paper account $100k → $354k in about a week (with two back-to-back red trades shown on the statement). Point: no rented MT4 servers needed, live data, no replay — concept and execution understanding, NOT a promise of what students will do.

## ICT Episode 29 — SMT Long on ES + Close Proximity Entries + The Quiet Day Before The Fed

**What this video is:** A Monday live-execution review. SMT picks the market to trade, the premium rule filters out a fake short, and "close proximity entries" get formally taught.

### Know the calendar's rhythm (why the day was slow)
- Next day: retail sales at 8:30 AM + **Fed chair speaks at 2:00 PM**.
- **The day BEFORE a Fed speech = lethargic, small range.** The algorithm coils. You CAN trade it, but it takes experience.
- And the Fed day itself? "Do not try to trade tomorrow. You'll probably hurt yourself." Volatility without structure until the event passes.

### SMT decided everything (ES over NQ)
- Bias was bullish (still drawing to NQ's 12,553 relative equal highs).
- At 10 AM: **NQ made a lower low. ES refused — twice.** ES saying "I'm not going lower" while its sister breaks down = bullish SMT divergence.
- Rule shape: bullish bias + one index refuses the lower low = trade the STRONGER one (he longed ES, zero NQ trades that day).
- Reminder: SMT only CONFIRMS an existing bias. He wasn't hunting shorts at all.

### The premium rule threw out a fake setup (again)
Early in the day there was a tempting short — swing low broken, FVG, retrace. But the gap sat in the WRONG half of the displacement leg (not in the premium). **Doesn't fit the model = doesn't exist.** No short taken.

### Close proximity entries (formally taught)
- Missed the exact FVG fill (his recording software hung)? Entering NEAR the gap — a tick or two away, or on the next touch — is fine. The model's edge is the CONTEXT, not tick-perfect entries.
- His fills took only ~1–2 handles of heat. The gap gave EIGHT candles of opportunity to get long in or near it. The market gives you time when the idea is right.
- Don't mourn missing the very low or the very high. He exited below the day's high and called it good.

### One more structure note
Second bullish confirmation before adding: another shift when the short-term highs broke, plus a second small FVG — that's where the add-on limit filled.

### The MT4 saga, closed

## ICT Episode 30 — Fed Speech Day: How Both Sides Got Cleared Before the Real Move

**What this video is:** The 2:00 PM Fed-chair day reviewed bottom-up (1-min → daily). A masterclass in pre-event manipulation — and the draw (12,553) finally paid.

### The anatomy of the pre-Fed manipulation (write this sequence down)
1. Ahead of 2:00: a **Judas pop that fell ONE TICK short** of the morning high (12,535.50 vs 12,535.75). Close-but-no-touch = bait.
2. Breakout buyers get triggered long → immediately taken into drawdown → **squeezed out below the relative equal lows** (the New York LUNCH lows).
3. Morning long holders — who trailed stops below lunch — get "raked across the coals," kicked out right before the real move so they can't profit from it.
4. Price dips into the imbalance BELOW the lunch lows (picking up the last orders)…
5. …then **at 3:00 PM the algorithm spools** straight up into the called draw: **12,553.25. Hammered it.**

Both sides cleared first. THEN delivery. That's the rigged rhythm of event days.

### Rules this day confirmed (already taught, now proven)
- **Bullish + lunch consolidation → expect the lunch lows to be swept → then the afternoon continuation.** Straight out of the earlier lessons.
- The model gave **NO short setup** on either sell-off — the sell-offs happened, but no valid formation appeared. When the market isn't going to deliver a direction, the algorithmic signatures simply don't print there. The model filters; trust the silence.
- An overhead FVG on the way UP is **not resistance** when the draw is above it — he annotated exactly that mid-trade ("this is not resistance, it lifts off from here"). Don't let a gap scare you out of a run toward liquidity.

### After the draw pays: re-map
Once 12,553 was hit, he immediately marked the NEXT draws up: the next old high (12,624), then the sloppier zone above. The map never ends — hit one magnet, name the next one.

### His own entry, honestly graded
He got in late ("poor entry, wasn't quick enough on the draw"), below the equal lows with his stop under the manipulation low — and it still paid ~$5k paper, because the CONTEXT was right. Close proximity beats missed entirely; context beats precision.

## ICT Episode 37 — NFP Eve: The Breaker, Bias With More Meat, and the Journaling Walkthrough

**What this video is:** Setting up NFP Friday as a STUDY day, a new PD array (the breaker) on the daily, and the fullest demonstration yet of how to journal a move.

### The breaker (new array, briefly)
- Shape: **low → high → lower low; take the HIGH in between and extend it out in time.** Price trading back down to it = support (bullish case).
- On the daily it lined up WITH the fair value gap — breaker + FVG together = the zone price bounced from to start the rally.

### Bias with more meat (daily chart reading)
- After a bottoming low, count the candles: nearly every day bullish = stay bullish until the opposing high is taken. One or two retracement days into a fair value gap along the way are LOGICAL, not bias-killers.
- Next draws named: the short-term high, then relative equal highs + the daily FVG above.
- The 8:30 rule for the next day: **if price is holding ABOVE the daily gap's low at 8:30 and the short-term high hasn't been taken yet — expect an ATTEMPT at that high.** It doesn't have to punch through; the attempt is the bias.

### NFP Friday = study day, never trade day
- NFP can be violent, choppy, or a total dud — and **precision drops precipitously** on those days even for him.
- He deliberately forced a small trade to demonstrate the point: got chopped. "If you have built-in advantages on normal days, why fight where you're disadvantaged?"
- Student drill INSTEAD of trading: before 8:30, decide which side's liquidity gets run. Then watch the 1-min and 5-min when it hits. Best free liquidity lesson there is.
- The Wednesday shutdown rule restated: nothing bagged by Wednesday NY session? Nothing else this week. The discipline is the reward.

### The spotter, again
- Midday Twitter drill: "find the 5-min FVG above price" → posted it → price ran straight to it. He's the spotter pointing at a level to STUDY, never a signal.
- His own wry observation: lately when he names a level publicly, price seems to rush straight to it.

### The journaling walkthrough (most complete version)
For every studied move, your chart should carry:
1. The marked order block, fair value gap, and entry/exit ARROWS (clearly graphical — not fake trades).
2. **Timing observations:** how many minutes from the gap being touched until delivery began (6 min there), and how long to reach the target (~20 min). Time-stamps train the internal clock.
3. Context flags: above/below the midnight open, lunch hour boundaries, which session.
4. Written observations phrased **as if you saw it coming** — borrowing HIS real-time call counts; that experience becomes yours through the journal.
5. Positive words only. Weekend reviews of the week and month, scrolling your own annotated charts.
- His bluntest line yet: **"You will do this or you will fail. Period. End of story."** Everyone who fails under his teaching starts failing right here — by skipping the journal.

## ICT Episode 38 — Scalping Model vs Day Trading, Changing Gears, and the Full Entry Menu

**What this video is:** The 4,070 draw never printed, the market flipped bullish, and he shows exactly how to CHANGE BIAS without panic. Plus: what "narrative" actually means.

### The model, properly classified
- **The YouTube model is a SCALPING model.** Day trading the full daily range = frame it on the 15-min + 5-min, and use the scalping model's entry as your trigger inside that bigger frame. Same tools, bigger canvas — scale it to any timeframe.

### How to change gears (bias management masterclass)
- He wanted 4,070 (sell side). Price refused, respected the daily FVG, gave NO short setup — then shifted structure UP.
- **Being wrong about a level with no trade on = zero loss.** No panic, no asking anyone's opinion, no revenge. The market simply voted; he re-read it.
- The tells that flipped him: multiple lows swept with NO valid short forming + the 15-min FVG holding as support + SMT (NQ lower low, ES refusing) = accumulation → afternoon bullish narrative.
- **Narrative, defined: what price SHOULD do, why, and what it will encounter along the way that PROVES the idea is actually underway.** Levels without narrative are guesses.

### The lunch tell (new)
- **Consolidation INTO lunch** = normal; expect the lunch lows swept after 1:00 then continuation.
- **RETRACEMENT into lunch** (actively pulling back instead of going sideways) = a FAST market "working through lunch" (old floor-trader behavior, now in the algorithm). Expect delivery to continue THROUGH the lunch hour — that's when his discount fill happened.

### His trade (the middle of the move)
- Waited out the whole morning ("just because you're in front of charts doesn't mean you deserve a trade").
- PM setup: dealing range fibbed (including the midnight-open logic for which low to use) → discount FVG → **bought the sell stops** below the short-term low (filled 4,110.25, a quarter point below the swept low) → partials at the old imbalance ("in case this is a failure swing, half comes off") → out before the target.
- Wrong about 4,070 AND wrong about previous day's high printing — **still took the fat middle of the move. "You don't need the highest high or the lowest low. The middle is good eating."**

### The entry menu (four ways to buy the same idea)
1. **OTE:** fib the impulse leg, buy the 62–79% retracement.
2. **Turtle soup + OTE:** the retrace VIOLATES a short-term low first (stop raid), then OTE entry.
3. **FVG touch:** buy the moment price touches the discount gap's upper edge. Stop below the low.
4. **Buy stop above the swing high** after the gap is touched (buying strength — his LEAST preferred, but valid).
All four require the same bias + narrative underneath. Pick the one that fits your personality.

### vs "liquidity voids" (a distinction he drew)
He does NOT require imbalances to fully rebalance (contrast with Chris Lorie's liquidity-void teaching — the one educator he respects). The question is always WHERE price is drawing to; the gap is a fair VALUE to transact at on the way, not a hole that must be filled edge-to-edge.

## ICT Episode 39 — Caleb's Model: One Setup, Five Points, Specific Times

**What this video is:** The exact stripped-down model he built for his son — the closest thing to a complete, times-and-rules recipe in the whole series.

### Time FIRST, always (the foundation)
- "**Price levels are useless until time is considered.**" The algorithm runs on time: time of day, day of week, week of month, month of year, seasonals.
- Pac-Man analogy: the daily range has PROGRAMMED boundaries like a video-game map. Price lives inside them — until manual intervention (FOMC, rate decisions) removes the walls and repricing goes wild.

### Caleb's model, complete
- **Windows: 8:30 AM, 9:30 AM (pick ONE morning shot), 1:30 PM, and the 3:00–4:00 market-on-close macro.**
- The setup (bearish version): above the midnight open + buy-side/short-term high swept at the window → swing low breaks WITH displacement → fair value gap forms → sell the retrace into it → target the named sell-side pool.
- **Take FIVE points. Then done.** Even when the framework offers 30. Five handles on one ES mini = $250; once a day = $1,250/week — more than his job pays.
- Daily cadence rules: win at 8:30 → skip 9:30, demo the afternoon. Miss 8:30 → try 9:30. Morning loss → allowed ONE afternoon trade. Never three live trades a day.
- Why 5 points: lowest honest threshold (below that, commissions eat you); it rewards him DAILY while learning; the discipline of leaving money on the table builds the confidence that kills FOMO, greed, and fear of the edge vanishing.

### The reversal tell (why he cut the short early)
- Price took sell side level after level after level… but barely MOVED lower. Little progress per purge = exhaustion.
- Then it traded UP THROUGH an up-close candle that institutional order flow said should hold as resistance. That violation = the day's run is flipping → he took 3 more off BEFORE the stop, kept a runner at better-than-breakeven. **Partial-out beats panic-close; let the stop do its job on the rest.**

### Honest context on the results
- Caleb: equity low $2,810 → $18,300+ in about four weeks (~400–500%). Real money, real broker.
- ICT's own framing: NOT typical, NOT a promise — the kid has a 30-year veteran sitting beside him as his internal dialogue, reading every pothole aloud. What transfers to students is the MODEL and the discipline, not the curve.
- His own loss this week: -5 points, publicly, no revenge trade. "A losing trade is a flat tire. Costs a little time and money. You still arrive."

### The graduation definition
You've graduated when you can watch a valid setup form, choose not to take it, watch it pay, and feel nothing. Independent thinking, fear and greed mastered, discipline unknown to most of social media. That's the destination: excellence, one step at a time.

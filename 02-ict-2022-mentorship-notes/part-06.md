## Live-Tape Video — FOMC Day on Micro NQ (recorded as it happened)

**What this video is:** He marks the map BEFORE the move on FOMC day, narrates it live (no replay button), and price walks the path. Ends with the fib and "8 setups in one leg."

### How FOMC (and NFP) days behave
- Announcement at 2:00 PM. Expect a **knee-jerk sequence: initial leg → second leg → FAKE-OUT leg → then the real setup.**
- The initial pricing usually finishes around **2:30** — that's when the real move tends to start.
- **Do NOT trade FOMC/NFP as a student.** Extremely risky. Study it live or in hindsight — it's the best free case study of liquidity being run to both ends of the range.

### New vocabulary — RUN vs SWEEP (he picks words on purpose)
- **Sweep** = price pokes just past a high/low, then REVERSES back into the range. (Shallow. Take-the-stops-and-leave.)
- **RUN** = price blasts THROUGH the level and keeps going. Expansion. Doesn't look back.
- His call: price would drop to the "checkered flag" zone (end of the road for bears), then **RUN Tuesday's high** — not sweep it. It did exactly that.

### The map he drew in advance
1. Post-FOMC drop into a lower fair value gap ("checkered flag" — the finish line for the bears).
2. Sell side resting just below it — a dip slightly past the zone is fine. Bodies respected the zone; wicks poked. Normal.
3. Turn → run Tuesday's high with force → continuation with every pullback into an imbalance or order block being a buy.

### Fib anchoring — wicks vs bodies (the rule, finally explicit)
- **Normal conditions: anchor on BODIES** (highest open/close to lowest open/close).
- **High-volatility events (FOMC, NFP): anchor on the EXTREMES (wicks)** — volatility makes the full range meaningful.
- That's the whole answer to "when does ICT use wicks?"

### Eight setups in one afternoon (the unique-model lesson)
He counted EIGHT valid trades in that one post-FOMC leg — FVG shorts, order block bounces, imbalance buys on the way up. The point is NOT to take all eight (you're not Spider-Man):
- Your unique model = the ONE of those eight that fits YOUR criteria. Different students legitimately pick different ones.
- Concepts are shared; setup selection is personal. He refuses to force everyone into one identical trade.

### Little confirmations along the way
- Institutional order flow check on the rally: every down-close candle SUPPORTED price on the way up = the run is healthy.
- Once the swing high broke after the turn, he projected the 2:00 high → post-2:30 low range for the upside target. Hit at 13,437.

## ICT Episode 24 — The Day the Setup DIDN'T Form (responsibility + the two entry frameworks)

**What this video is:** The most important "boring" episode. The model kept him OUT of a bad trade, he draws the two entry diagrams cleanly, and he lays down the responsibility law.

### The model proved itself by staying silent
- His community post said: "study this, I am watching this" — a fair value gap where a 9:30 Judas dip might set up a long. That is a STUDY prompt, never a signal. ("Study / watch / observe" are not code words.)
- Price DID dip below the old low (sell stops taken)… but then: **no displacement back above the low. No energetic move. It just melted.**
- No displacement = no trigger = no fair value gap to buy = **NO TRADE.** He took nothing. The move ran off without him — and that's the model WORKING: sound logic keeps you out of button-pushing losses.
- A missed move is a loss too — but only a mental one. Don't amplify it. "Don't live in your losses."

### WHERE setups form — the two diagrams (write these down)

**Framework 1 — Relative equal highs version (bearish; flip for longs):**
1. Price runs UP through relative equal highs.
2. The moment it's above them, look back left: find the **nearest short-term low. That low is the trigger.**
3. Nothing exists until that low breaks WITH displacement. (Just dipping back under the highs is NOT it.)
4. The displacement leg = the foundation. Hunt the fair value gap inside THAT leg → short the retrace into it.

**Framework 2 — Old fair value gap version (bearish; flip for longs):**
1. An old FVG sits ABOVE the market. You're bearish but expect price to reach up into it.
2. **Treat the LOW of that gap exactly like relative equal highs.** Price trading anywhere INTO the gap counts — no full fill needed.
3. Then same trigger logic: nearest short-term low breaks with displacement → FVG in the displacement leg → entry.

That's the whole entry playbook for the model. Two pictures. Nothing else.

### The responsibility law (his hardest sermon)
- He never gives entries, stops, or targets — not on YouTube, not to paying students. Everything is demo/paper framing. If you push a button, the result is YOURS — win or lose. He will never co-sign anyone's ruin.
- Journal assignment, dated: write "**I promise that as long as I'm doing this, I will ENDURE losses.**" Endure — not be defeated by. Every trade opens as a loser (spread/commission); trading is managing your way out of losses, every single time.
- Impulse winners are the most dangerous: get lucky once and you'll credit skill, then hand it all back. Self-control is the whole game.
- NFP Friday: usually volatile, sometimes a total dud. Same caution as FOMC.
- Mindset filter he wants: are you here to learn, be entertained, or be disruptive? Only the first group makes it.

## ICT Episode 25 — Rebalancing the Previous Day + The Premium Entry Rule

**What this video is:** The "indecisive" daily candle decoded, the previous-day's-low rebalance target, and a hard-line filter: only sell from a premium of the displacement leg.

### Rebalancing a big down day (new targeting idea)
- Monday = huge down candle (one-sided delivery). What does Tuesday do? Rallies back up to **Friday's low** — the previous day's low BEFORE the big drop. That level rebalances Monday's whole range.
- On the daily, Tuesday looks like a confused doji. On the intraday it's a clean Power Three: 8:30 open → Judas rally up to Friday's low at 9:30 → model setup → distribution down all day.
- **Study the last 3 days' open/high/low/close, always** (his original baby pips "laboratory" — nobody ever found the FVG from it on their own).

### The sequence tells you the bias is intact
- Bearish bias + **buy side ran FIRST** (before any old low was taken) = confirms bearish. They grabbed the buy stops to sell to.
- If the sell side had been taken first and THEN it rallied — that's a different story. Order of liquidity purges = information.

### THE PREMIUM RULE (hard-line filter — write in stone)
Once you have the displacement leg, fib it high-to-low:
- **Bearish: your fair value gap entry must sit in the PREMIUM (above the 50%) of that leg.**
- A gap sitting in the discount half is NOT your trade — skip it, keep dropping timeframes (5→4→3→2→1) until you find one in the premium (his was on the 2-min — "the unicorn": FVG + imbalance + short-term low taken).
- Bullish = mirror: buy only from the discount half.
This one rule filters out a pile of losing entries by itself.

### Stop placement + when to move it
- Initial stop: 1–2 ticks above the candle that CREATES the fair value gap. Scared? Use the swing high — and trade micros so the wider stop is affordable ($5/handle vs $50).
- **Do not trail early.** Moving the stop tight right away strangles the trade — that's the #1 way marginal winners become stop-outs.
- Only move the stop after a LARGER structural break (the next low gets taken out). Logic: once that low broke, price shouldn't come all the way back — if it does, you were wrong anyway.
- Getting out of marginal winners early and strangling positions are the two habits that filter most people out of this business.

### Odds and ends
- Limit orders CAN fill during lunch (his filled ~12:27) — you just don't initiate new decisions then.
- Asia session: the model rarely forms well there. If you must: yen pairs, AUD, NZD on 1–5 min charts. And no, Wellington doesn't start the day — **the algorithm runs on New York midnight. Period.**
- Seasonals again: May weakness in indices ("I don't expect snow in Maryland on July 4th" — that's what a seasonal tendency is).
- **The six-month path:** ~3 months of daily back-testing + 2–3 months of forward testing in demo = you'll have found your model. This YouTube model is the SIMPLEST of his 12 — built so his daughter could run it. Any concept (breaker, mitigation block, stop run) can be stripped down the same way, but you don't need them.

## Live-Tape Video — Counter-Trend Long Against Daily Bias (ES, narrated live)

**What this video is:** A quick live demo of trading AGAINST the daily bias — and the exact conditions that make that acceptable.

### When counter-trend is justified (his checklist that day)
1. **Sell side already taken** — relative equal lows swept, yesterday's low taken.
2. Price dipped three times with NO downside follow-through — the market refused to go lower.
3. Price had already traded into the fair value gap below.
4. Clear opposing draw: buy-side liquidity overhead (shorts' stops).
→ The purge is DONE, so the next draw is the other side. That's the only reason to fight the daily bias — and even then, expectations stay modest.

### Target logic: need vs want
- Best case: the high of the day (4051). **But the trade doesn't NEED that to work.**
- The REAL target = the nearer buy-side pool. He took the bulk (4 of 6) there with a market order. Plan the trade around the target you're confident in; treat the extension as a bonus.

### Live management shown
- Entries: FVG first (biggest position), then adds at the bullish order block — pyramid again, big → small.
- Stop: below the low that "should not be traded back into," which was ALSO the lower edge of the FVG + old structure. Stops live where the logic dies, not at round dollar amounts.
- **Not in a hurry to move the stop up.** Breakeven only came late, after the move was well underway. The last 2 contracts got tripped out at the raised stop — fine, the trade was already funded.
- Watched CANDLES, not the P&L flicker ("that number is the distraction").
- Retail saw the drift as a bear flag and shorted the rebalanced imbalance — he was buying, targeting exactly those sellers' stops.

### Fun vocab
- **"Caffeine bar"** = a big energetic green candle when you're bullish. You want to see those confirming you.
- Down-close candles supporting each leg up = institutional order flow saying the rally is healthy (same check as always).

## The scaling plan

**Option 1 — start with 1 account ($99.45).** For newer traders. Pass eval → get funded →
first payout (up to $2,000) → use payout money to buy more accounts → repeat. His framing:
first goal is NOT $10K/month, it's **proving you can get paid once**, then scaling what
already worked.

**Option 2 — start with 5 accounts ($348.85, 5th free).** For proven traders. Copy-trade
the same strategy across all 5. "Five accounts doesn't mean I suddenly become 5x more
aggressive. I'm just multiplying something that already works."

**The math he shows:** 25-point base hit on NQ = $500/account × 5 accounts = $2,500 per
trade. Four winning 25-pt trades = $10K/month (5 accounts × $2,000 payout cap).

Both options: "Neither changes how I trade. The only difference is how quickly I want to
scale." Base hits, protect capital, scale like a business.

## Honest context (mine, not PJ's)

- The $10K math assumes the trades **win**. A 25-pt stop on 1 NQ contract is $500/account —
  copy-traded, losses multiply exactly like wins. Four full stops ≈ one account's $2,000
  max loss.
- It also assumes all 5 accounts pass eval and stay funded. Eval fees are the known cost;
  blown accounts are re-bought.
- Nitro's **trailing drawdown when funded** is the classic account-killer rule — flagged in
  the glossary.
- PJ earns from code "PJ" — normal for the space, but the incentive exists. Prices/deals
  were "this week only" as of the release.
- This is the first document where PJ implies a concrete income figure. His own earlier
  rule: no promises about what the model produces. Treat $10K/month as the marketing
  scenario, not the expectation.

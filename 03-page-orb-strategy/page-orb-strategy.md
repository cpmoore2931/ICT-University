# Page's Own Model — The 8:00 AM Gold ORB

This is **Page's personal strategy**. She built it herself. It is not PJ's model.
She also trades with PJ (in his Discord), so both live in this project — keep them
separate. When she asks about "my ORB," this doc is the source.

Instrument in the example: **MGC (Micro Gold)**, 1-minute chart.
All times below are **ET** unless marked otherwise.

> Where this doc says *unclear* or *open question*, that part has not been pinned
> down yet. Do not fill it in with a guess.

---

## 1. Pre-market prep

1. Mark the highs and lows on the **1-minute** chart.
2. Zoom out to the **15m and 1h**. Mark fair value gaps and zones price will
   likely draw to. Then zoom back into the 1m.
3. Draw a box around the **first 15 minutes** of each open:
   **8:00, 8:30, 9:00, and 12:00.**

That last step is what builds the ORB.

---

## 2. The ORB itself

- Built at **8:00 AM**, off the **high and low of the first 15–30 minutes**.
- Origin: she had a dream where she told herself, *"Trade gold 8:00 AM orb."*
  She has been trading it since.
- The **midrange (50%) of the orb is the key line.** It decides the whole trade.

---

## 3. The three conditions — confirmed from her marked-up chart

She labeled these herself. All three must be true.

1. **MUST SWEEP LOW.** Price has to take out the low. This happens in the
   **8:30–9:00 wait window**. She does not trade this leg — she watches it.
2. **MUST STAY ABOVE THE MID RANGE OF ORB.** After the sweep, price comes back
   up and has to hold above the orb's 50%. If it loses the mid, the setup is dead.
3. **TARGET = THE PREVIOUS HIGH.** Not a guess, not a runner. The high she
   marked in pre-market prep is the target.

So the shape is: **sweep the low → reclaim and hold the mid → run to the
previous high.**

## 4. Timing

| Window | What she does |
|---|---|
| 8:00–8:15/8:30 | Build the orb |
| 8:30–9:00 | Wait and watch. The sweep should happen here. |
| ~9:30–10:40 | The trade window. Mid must hold. |
| 9:40–12:00 | Return to the orb / entry can come anywhere in here |

---

## 5. One-and-done rule ✅ confirmed 8/17

**After one trade closes in profit — even a small one — she is done for the day.**
She does not trade again. She watches the rest of the move to learn and to build
confidence.

This is a keeper. It matches "base hits pay the bills" and "you cannot go broke
taking profits." Do not talk her out of it.

**But it has a consequence:** if she only gets one trade, that one trade has to be
priced well. One-and-done plus a late entry = a tiny day, every day. The rule is
not the problem. The entry price is.

---

## 6. Entry — the weak point (her own words: "my biggest weakness")

- Sometimes she waits for a **retest**. Sometimes she does not.
- She skips the retest when the draw is obvious.

**The gap: the rules say what must be TRUE, not what price to BUY.**
On 8/17 she filled at 4459.3 with the target at ~4460.86 — about **1.5 points**.
Same setup entered near the mid (~4451) is about **10 points**.

### Proposed entry rule (being tested, not yet confirmed by Page)

Treat the ORB as **internal → external** — the same frame PJ teaches:

- **External / target** = the previous high (she already has this)
- **Internal / entry** = the orb midrange (she has this too, but was only using
  it as a filter, not as the entry)

Sequence:
1. Sweep the low
2. Price pushes back up through the mid
3. That push leaves a **1-minute FVG** behind it
4. **Entry = pullback into that gap, or the retest of the mid**
5. Stop below the sweep low or below the gap
6. Target the previous high

### The 5-second pre-click test
Points to stop vs. points to target. **If target < 2× stop, no trade.** On 8/17
this test would have blocked the 4459.3 fill.

## 8. How she classifies errors

Every mistake goes in one of three buckets:

1. **Method failure** — the model was executed wrong, or the model itself was wrong.
2. **Psychological failure** — she knew better and did it anyway.
3. **Probability failure** — nothing was wrong. Good setup, bad outcome. Not an
   error to fix.

8/17's late entry was a **platform failure** — none of the three.
**Open question: fourth bucket, or folded into method?**
